ESSAY · 2026

I Failed Before I Turned 17

I'm Bhavy Chaudhary. 17. From Aligarh. I built NearBase — a hyperlocal pre-order and pickup platform. I launched it. I had a team. The product worked. But the market didn't respond the way I hoped.

The idea was simple: people don't always want delivery. Sometimes they just want the order ready when they reach the shop. We built it. It worked. And then… silence. No revenue. No pull. We had built a solution, but we hadn't earned the one thing that makes local commerce work — trust.

Here's what the failure taught me. One: a working product without trust is nothing. Two: distribution beats features — nobody finds you just because you built well. Three: timing and behaviour matter more than code; people don't change habits for a slightly better option. Four: documenting failure honestly is worth more than performing success.

I failed before I turned 17 — not an exam, not a relationship, but a startup. That failure taught me more than success could.

ESSAY · 2026

Why UP Is India's Next Startup Capital

Everyone looks at Bengaluru, Mumbai, Delhi. They're looking at the past. India's next founders won't only come from metros — they'll come from Aligarh, Gonda, Iglas.

Small-town founders understand real India because they live its problems. UP has the youth, the hunger, and problems worth solving at scale. What we call "disadvantages" — limited networks, no playbook, nobody watching — are actually unfair advantages. We learn to build with nothing, which means we can build anywhere.

Aligarh already shaped founders like Vijay Shekhar Sharma. There's a new generation coming — and I'm part of it. We're adding to the list, and making sure UP becomes the state every young founder wants to build from.
